# Problem 11.1: M7 synthesis-only self-consistency is a partial fix

**Date:** Sept 14, 2026
**Where seen:** `docs/outputs/11_analysis_30q_m7_synth_only.txt`

## The M7 change

Applied self-consistency to the synthesis step only. Sample the final 
synthesis N=5 times at temperature=0.7, use the modal answer, and set 
overall confidence = min(hop_confidence) × (modal_count / N).

Rationale: cheapest first. If synthesis-only fixes calibration, we 
avoid the 5× cost of sub-answer self-consistency.

## Result

Calibration buckets (30 questions):

| Bucket | n | Correct | Accuracy | M5 (100q) accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [0.0, 0.5) | 12 | 3 | 25% | 20% |
| [0.5, 0.7) | 2 | 2 | 100% | 75% |
| [0.7, 0.9) | 0 | - | - | 50% |
| **[0.9, 1.01)** | **16** | **11** | **69%** | **68%** |

Fraction of questions receiving high confidence dropped from 72% (M5) 
to 53% (M7) — better. But the [0.9, 1.0] bucket accuracy stayed at 
69% — the confidently-wrong cases are still confidently wrong at 
almost the same rate.

Accuracy on 30 questions: 53% contains-gold (M5 baseline: 58%). 
Within noise for n=30.

## Diagnosis

The failure cases show a specific pattern. In "David Weissman / 
Evolution / Nicolas Cage" the sub-answers gave the wrong entity 
(the film title instead of a person). The synthesizer had no 
disagreement to detect — all 5 samples produced the same wrong 
answer because they were all conditioned on the same wrong sub-answers.

**The calibration failure lives in the sub-answers, not the synthesis.** 
When sub-answers are already wrong-but-consistent, no amount of 
downstream sampling detects that. Synthesis self-consistency only 
catches cases where the synthesizer is uncertain about how to combine 
correct sub-answers.

## Options going forward

1. **Escalate to full self-consistency** — apply sampling to 
   sub-answers too. 5× cost. If sub-answers disagree across samples, 
   that's the missing signal.
2. **Retrieval-score-based confidence** — use the FAISS similarity 
   score of the top passage per hop as a confidence signal. Free, 
   grounded in the retrieval quality rather than the LLM.
3. **LLM-as-judge calibration** — separate LLM call to score whether 
   the passages actually contain the claimed answer. Adds cost but 
   grounds confidence in the passages.
4. **Combined signal** — weighted mix of (a) sub-answer self-consistency, 
   (b) retrieval scores, (c) hop confidence floor. Best expected 
   quality, highest cost.

## Decision

Do option 2 next (retrieval score) — free, doesn't require sampling, 
tests whether retrieval quality alone predicts correctness. If it 
does, cheapest calibration fix. If it doesn't, escalate to option 1.

## Takeaway

Self-consistency at the wrong layer doesn't help. Consistency is only 
a signal if there is real disagreement to detect, and the point of 
disagreement has to be where the failure lives. Debugging calibration 
means finding *which stage* is confidently wrong.
