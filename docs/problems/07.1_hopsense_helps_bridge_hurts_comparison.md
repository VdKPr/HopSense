# Problem 03: HopSense helps bridge questions, mildly hurts comparisons

**Date:** Sept 14, 2026
**Where seen:** `docs/outputs/07_analysis_100q.txt`

## The observation

Contains-gold accuracy by HotpotQA question type on 100 questions:

| Type | n | Naive | HopSense | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bridge | 79 | 33% | 57% | **+24 pp** |
| comparison | 21 | 67% | 62% | -5 pp |

Bridge questions dominate the dataset (79%), so HopSense's overall 
gain is real (+18 pp on contains-gold). But comparisons regress.

## Why bridges win under HopSense

Bridge = "X of the Y of Z" style. Requires chained lookups:
- "Who directed the film starring [actor]?" needs: 
  → find film starring actor → find film's director

Naive retrieval mushes both entities into one query vector and 
often retrieves only one hop's supporting evidence. HopSense 
retrieves per hop → both hops land.

## Why comparisons lose under HopSense

Comparison = "Who is older, A or B?" Both entities already in the 
question. A single retrieval on the full question naturally surfaces 
BOTH A and B's pages (because both names are in the query embedding).

HopSense's decomposition creates two sub-questions and retrieves 
twice, but the synthesizer must then re-derive the comparison — 
losing information or occasionally misreasoning about direction 
(who is older vs younger).

## Options

1. **Skip decomposition for comparison questions.** Route them to 
   naive RAG. Requires a classifier: comparison vs bridge.
2. **Improve synthesizer prompt** for comparisons — force explicit 
   date/number extraction before comparing.
3. **Accept the trade-off.** Bridges are 4× more common; +24 pp on 
   bridges is worth -5 pp on comparisons.

## Decision

For now: option 3, accept the trade-off. Note it in results.
Explore option 1 later — could push overall accuracy higher.

## Takeaway

Multi-hop decomposition helps chained retrieval but adds no value 
(and may hurt) when the original query already contains both 
entities. Worth measuring properly before deciding whether to route 
comparison-style questions through decomposition at all.
